# Source research and quality

## Evidence hierarchy

Adapt the hierarchy to the domain. Default order:

1. Official specification, standard, regulator, or documentation.
2. Primary research, original paper, RFC, or official repository.
3. Established textbook or university course.
4. Material by a recognised practitioner with direct evidence.
5. Community discussion for operational experience or counterexamples.

For medical, legal, financial, safety, or regulatory topics, prioritise current authoritative sources and explicitly state jurisdiction, date, and uncertainty.

## Selection test

Open every selected source and answer:

- Does it directly teach or substantiate the node outcome?
- Is the author or institution identifiable?
- Is the material current enough for the claim?
- Is it accessible to this learner?
- Does it add value beyond another selected source?
- Is the expected effort proportionate to the node's importance?

Reject sources selected only because their title resembles the topic.

## Tiers

- **Foundation** — Minimum coherent material needed to perform the node outcomes.
- **Deepening** — Adds mechanism, nuance, alternatives, or primary evidence.
- **Reference** — Kept for lookup rather than sequential study.

Use adaptive counts. A narrow node may need one authoritative source; a contested or multidisciplinary node may need several independent sources.

## Research record

For every selected source preserve title, URL, type, reason, level, language, expected effort, and verification date. Distinguish source fact, inference from multiple sources, and pedagogical recommendation.

## Link verification

Run `scripts/check_links.py`. A successful HTTP response does not prove relevance; manual opening and content inspection remain required.

Do not fabricate paywall status, duration, edition, publication date, or availability. If a source cannot be opened, replace it or label it unverified.
